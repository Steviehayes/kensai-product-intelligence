# Reference: EU / UK nutrition & health claims (condensed)
_For assessing whether a product's on-pack/marketing claims are permitted._

Governing rules: EU Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006 (EU Register of authorised
claims). Post-Brexit, Great Britain maintains its own GB Nutrition & Health
Claims (NHC) Register, wording largely mirroring the EU but managed separately.

## Examples of AUTHORISED claims (permitted wording)
- "Vitamin B12 contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism."
- "Vitamin B12 contributes to normal functioning of the nervous system."
- "Folate contributes to normal blood formation."
- "Vitamin D contributes to the normal function of the immune system."
- "Iron contributes to normal formation of red blood cells and haemoglobin."
(Each is only permitted if the product contains a significant amount of the nutrient.)

## NOT permitted / non-compliant wording
- "Boosts your immune system" (vague/absolute; not the authorised wording)
- Any claim implying prevention, treatment or cure of disease
  (e.g. "cures colds", "detoxes the body") — prohibited on food supplements.

## Note
A claim being scientifically plausible does NOT make it legal to print —
only the exact authorised wording (with conditions of use met) may be used.
